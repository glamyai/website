FINANCIAL MODEL & RESOURCE PLANNING

A bottom-up five-year model connecting Glamy's commercial validation, distribution strategy, operational expansion and capital requirements.

The model is built from operating assumptions rather than a top-down revenue target. Hospitality is modeled as the initial deployment wedge, with expansion into other service-intensive operations as the architecture proves transferable.

The base case assumes three things must become true: distribution scales beyond founder-led sales, customer value expands with operational depth, and the architecture transfers beyond hospitality.

Hospitality is modeled as Glamy's initial deployment wedge, not its five-year market boundary.

The first phase uses hospitality to validate the deployment model in an environment with high interaction density, cross-functional operations, context-dependent decisions and immediate operational consequences.

The model assumes that, once repeatability is demonstrated, the same operational architecture begins expanding into other service-intensive organizations with similar underlying characteristics.

By Year 3, revenue outside hospitality becomes a critical validation point for the platform thesis. If the architecture does not transfer beyond hospitality, the broader market assumptions would need to be reassessed.

MODEL INTERPRETATION

The model is designed around three sequential proofs.

First, Glamy must prove that organizations will pay for operational deployments and that those deployments create measurable value.

Second, it must prove that customer acquisition and deployment can become repeatable rather than remaining dependent on founder-led execution.

Third, it must prove that the underlying operational architecture transfers from hospitality into other service-intensive operations.

The financial trajectory is therefore not based on hospitality alone. Hospitality provides the initial environment in which the deployment model is validated. The larger outcome depends on Glamy demonstrating that the same architectural pattern exists across a broader set of service-intensive organizations.

The model also does not require a single very large network-scale customer to achieve the base case. Large deployments across hundreds or thousands of operational units represent potential upside rather than an embedded assumption.

SCENARIO & FORWARD-LOOKING DISCLAIMER

This model represents a management base case, not a guarantee of future performance.

It is intended to make Glamy's operating assumptions explicit and testable. Actual performance may vary materially depending on customer conversion, pricing, deployment time, partner productivity, vertical expansion, retention, AI infrastructure costs and future financing conditions.

Large network-scale deployments are not required for the base case and are not explicitly modeled as breakout events. Such deployments could create material upside, while slower distribution or unsuccessful cross-vertical expansion would create downside.

The purpose of the model is not to predict a precise five-year outcome. It is to show how commercial validation, operational expansion, distribution leverage and cross-vertical transferability could translate into a scalable economic model.
