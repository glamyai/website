# Glamy Market Opportunity

## Methodology

Glamy's market opportunity is modeled primarily using a bottom-up approach.

Rather than applying an arbitrary percentage to a broad AI or software market, the model starts with the number of organizations that match Glamy's operational profile and estimates the recurring software revenue that those organizations could support.

The model separates three types of inputs:

- External data — organization counts, hotel capacity, enterprise-size distributions and market structure published by statistical authorities and industry sources.
- Glamy assumptions — ICP filters, normalized annual contract values and extrapolations where directly comparable public data is unavailable.
- Independent market signals — third-party forecasts used to validate the broader shift toward agentic and operational AI. These signals are not included directly in Glamy's bottom-up market-size calculation.

All figures are rounded and should be interpreted as modeled market opportunities rather than forecasts of Glamy's future revenue.

## €8B TAM

### Global service-intensive operations

Glamy's Total Addressable Market represents service organizations where customer conversations can trigger operational decisions and real-world actions.

The core economic pattern is:

> Conversation → Context → Judgment → Organizational Action

The initial model includes organizations across accommodation, passenger travel and transport, travel and reservation services, facility and distributed support operations, and adjacent service-intensive categories where the same underlying operational architecture can apply.

### Bottom-up calculation

> ~315,000 addressable organizations
> ×
> ~€25,000 normalized blended annual contract value
> = ~€7.9B
> Rounded: ~€8B TAM

### What is externally sourced?

Eurostat Structural Business Statistics provide the underlying reference for European enterprise structure by economic activity and enterprise size.

Source: [Eurostat — Structural Business Statistics](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/structural-business-statistics)

In 2024, the EU business economy contained approximately 251,000 medium-sized enterprises (50–249 employees) and 55,000 large enterprises (250+ employees). Although these organizations represented only around 1% of enterprises, they accounted for a disproportionately large share of employment and turnover.

Source: [Eurostat — Enterprise size structure, 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20251209-2)

### What is modeled?

The 315,000 global addressable organization count is a Glamy modeling assumption, not a statistic published by Eurostat.

It extrapolates from observed European enterprise structure to a global service-intensive organization universe while excluding large portions of the service economy that do not exhibit the operational characteristics Glamy targets.

The €25K blended ACV is also a Glamy normalized long-term pricing assumption.

For transparency, a reasonable sensitivity range around the current model is approximately:

> €4.5B–€13.5B, with ~€8B used as the base case.

## €1.4B SAM

### Mid & upper-mid service operations

The Serviceable Available Market narrows the global opportunity to Glamy's initial service verticals and organizational profile.

The current core includes:

- Hospitality & accommodation
- Passenger travel & transport
- Travel & reservation services
- Facility & distributed support operations

The focus is not simply on organizations classified as “services.” Glamy targets organizations with sufficient operational complexity for conversational context to affect fulfillment, coordination or decision-making.

### Bottom-up calculation

> ~45,000 addressable organizations
> ×
> ~€30,000 normalized annual contract value
> = ~€1.35B
> Rounded: ~€1.4B SAM

### Why mid & upper-mid?

The choice reflects both operational economics and Glamy's initial go-to-market thesis.

Very small organizations generally have lower operational complexity and lower potential contract values. At the opposite extreme, very large enterprises can support substantial value but often introduce longer procurement, governance and integration cycles.

The initial opportunity therefore concentrates on organizations large enough to experience significant operational complexity while remaining commercially accessible.

Eurostat data supports the broader economic relevance of this segment. In 2022, medium enterprises represented only 0.8% of EU enterprises but 15% of employment, while large enterprises represented 0.2% of enterprises but 35% of employment and 50% of net turnover.

Source: [Eurostat — SME / enterprise-size economic contribution](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20231212-1)

Tourism data also illustrates the scale of the underlying operational sectors. Eurostat reports approximately 2.1 million people working in accommodation, 1.8 million in transport and 0.4 million in travel agencies and tour operators in the referenced EU tourism-industry dataset.

Source: [Eurostat — Tourism industries](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20240529-1)

### What is modeled?

The ~45K organization universe is a Glamy model, constructed by filtering relevant service sectors for organizational scale and operational fit.

It should not be interpreted as an official Eurostat count of “Glamy-addressable companies.”

Similarly, the €30K normalized ACV represents the estimated mature annual software value of a Glamy deployment across this mixed service-organization portfolio rather than Glamy's current pricing.

## €100M SOM

### 200+ room European hospitality

The Serviceable Obtainable Market represents Glamy's first vertical wedge: operationally complex European hotels.

Room count is used as a proxy for operational complexity, rather than as a statement about hotel quality or brand positioning.

The current threshold is:

> 200+ rooms

This deliberately excludes the majority of Europe's small accommodation properties and concentrates the model on hotels where guest volume, departmental coordination and operational complexity are materially higher.

### Bottom-up model

The current model estimates approximately:

> ~5,900–8,100 European 200+ room properties
> ×
> €15,000 normalized annual ACV per property
> = approximately €89M–€122M annual opportunity
> Base case ≈ €105M
> Rounded conservatively for the deck: ~€100M SOM

### Important modeling note

Public European accommodation statistics do not consistently publish a single EU-wide count of hotels with exactly 200+ rooms.

The model therefore begins with published hotel-capacity and size-class data and estimates the 200+ subset from the available size distributions.

Sources: [Eurostat — Accommodation capacity / size classes](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Tourism_statistics_-_annual_results_for_the_accommodation_sector) · [Eurostat — Tourism accommodation methodology](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/EN/tour_occ_esms.htm)

This is a Glamy modeling step, not an official Eurostat statistic.

Country-level sources are used as additional calibration points where more granular capacity data is available.

For example, Italy reported 32,943 hotel establishments in 2024, demonstrating the highly fragmented nature of the underlying hotel universe.

Sources: [ISTAT — Tourism flows and accommodation structure, 2024](https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/i-flussi-turistici-anno-2024/) · [ISTAT — Accommodation establishment capacity survey](https://www.istat.it/informazioni-sulla-rilevazione/capacita-degli-esercizi-ricettivi/)

### Normalized hospitality ACV

The model uses:

> €15K / property / year
> Sensitivity range: approximately €12K–€18K

This is a Glamy pricing assumption for a mature deployment in a 200+ room property, not a claimed industry average.

External hospitality-software pricing is used only as a reasonableness check.

For example, Viqal publicly prices its hotel AI/messaging product according to room count and moves properties/groups above 500 rooms to custom enterprise pricing. Canary similarly uses custom pricing for its hospitality platform, including guest messaging, AI responses and automated service-ticket creation.

Sources: [Viqal — Pricing](https://www.viqal.com/pricing) · [Canary Technologies — Pricing](https://secure.canarytechnologies.com/pricing) · [hotelkit — Pricing / FAQ](https://hotelkit.net/faq/)

These benchmarks support room count as a commercially relevant pricing variable, but do not directly establish Glamy's €15K ACV assumption.

## ~€40M Initial Beachhead

### Italy · Türkiye · Spain · Greece

Glamy's initial commercial focus is narrower than its European hospitality SOM.

The initial beachhead concentrates on 200+ room hotels in Italy, Türkiye, Spain and Greece, with particular emphasis on mid and upper-mid market organizations where Glamy expects the strongest combination of:

- operational complexity
- meaningful customer-interaction volume
- accessible decision-making
- sufficient software budget
- lower organizational friction than global enterprise deployments

Sources: [T.C. Ministry of Culture and Tourism — Accommodation statistics methodology](https://yigm.ktb.gov.tr/TR-268376/metaveri.html) · [T.C. Ministry of Culture and Tourism — Monthly accommodation statistics](https://engelsiz.ktb.gov.tr/TR-407335/2025-yili-aylik-bultenler.html) · [Spain hotel size distribution — Eurostat-derived](https://www.statista.com/statistics/687488/number-of-hotels-and-similar-accommodation-by-number-of-rooms-in-spain/) · [Institute for Tourism Research and Forecasts — Greek Hospitality Performance 2024](https://www.itep.gr/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/AS_2024_site_public_en_revised.pdf) · [Greece in Numbers — Hotel capacity by size](https://www.greeceinnumbers.gr/en-gb/Sectors%20and%20Enterprises/Tourism/Capacity)

The current modeled opportunity is approximately:

> ~€40M

This figure should be interpreted as an initial go-to-market opportunity, not as the ultimate size of Glamy's hospitality market.

The distinction is deliberate:

> SOM ≈ €100M — serviceable European hospitality opportunity.
> Initial Beachhead ≈ €40M — first geographic and commercial focus.

## Independent Market Signals

The bottom-up calculations above deliberately do not use broad AI-market forecasts.

However, independent research provides evidence that the underlying enterprise-software market is moving toward the architecture Glamy is built around.

Source: [Gartner — Agentic AI in enterprise applications / $450B+ long-term scenario](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-26-gartner-predicts-40-percent-of-enterprise-apps-will-feature-task-specific-ai-agents-by-2026-up-from-less-than-5-percent-in-2025)

### $234B enterprise software spend exposed to agentic AI

Gartner estimates that up to $234B of enterprise application spending will be exposed to agentic arbitrage by 2030, representing roughly 20% of enterprise application SaaS spending.

Gartner describes agentic arbitrage as AI agents completing work across multiple systems, reducing the need for users to interact with multiple traditional software interfaces.

Source: [Gartner — $234B enterprise application spend exposed to agentic AI](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-01-gartner-says-us-dollars-234-billion-in-enterprise-application-software-spend-is-at-risk-from-agentic-artificial-intelligence)

### $985B projected agentic AI software spend

Gartner separately projects agentic AI software spending to reach $985B by 2030, growing at a 62.7% CAGR from 2025–2030.

Source: [Gartner — Agentic AI software spending forecast, $985B by 2030](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7455226)

This figure is not Glamy's TAM. It is included only as evidence of the broader technological and software-spending transition.

## Assumptions & limitations

This market model combines externally sourced market structure data with Glamy-specific assumptions. Organization counts, hotel capacity and enterprise-size distributions are grounded in third-party sources where available. Addressability filters, global extrapolations and normalized ACVs are Glamy assumptions and are explicitly identified as such. Public datasets do not provide every intersection required by Glamy's ICP — particularly the exact number of 200+ room hotels across Europe — so ranges and transparent modeling assumptions are used rather than presenting estimated values as reported statistics. Market opportunity represents potential recurring software revenue and should not be interpreted as a revenue forecast or expected market share.
